Conclusion:

In this project, customer segmentation was performed on the SmartCart e-commerce dataset using unsupervised machine learning techniques. 
The data was cleaned, preprocessed, standardized, and analyzed to identify meaningful customer groups based on purchasing behavior, 
income, engagement, and demographic attributes.

K-Means clustering was applied after feature scaling, and the optimal number of clusters was evaluated using the Elbow Method, 
Silhouette Score & Dendrogram. Although different evaluation techniques suggested different cluster values, 4 clusters were selected because 
they provided the most interpretable and actionable customer segments. The selected K=4 model achieved a Silhouette Score of 0.379, 
indicating a reasonable level of cluster separation and cohesion.

The identified customer segments were:

1. Budget Family Shoppers: Customers with lower income and spending patterns, generally purchasing fewer products and requiring value-based offers.
2. Premium Loyal Customers: High-income customers with the highest spending and frequent purchases across multiple channels, representing valuable customers 
			    for retention strategies.
3. Low-Value Solo Shoppers: Customers with lower engagement and spending, who can be targeted with personalized recommendations and promotional campaigns.
4. High-Value Responsive Customers: High-income customers with strong purchasing behavior and the highest response rate, making them ideal targets for 
				    focused marketing campaigns.

The clustering analysis helps SmartCart understand customer behavior and enables data-driven decisions such as personalized marketing, 
customer retention strategies, loyalty programs, and targeted promotions.

Overall, this project demonstrates how unsupervised learning can transform raw customer data into meaningful business insights and improve 
customer relationship management.
